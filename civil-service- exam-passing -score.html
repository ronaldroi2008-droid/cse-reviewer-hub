Civil Service Exam Passing Score 2026: What Score Do You Need to Pass?

Many Civil Service Exam examinees ask the same question before taking the examination: What score do you need to pass the Civil Service Exam?

Understanding the passing score can help candidates set realistic goals, prepare more effectively, and reduce exam-day anxiety. This guide explains how the Civil Service Exam passing score works and what examinees should know before taking the test.

What Is the Passing Score for the Civil Service Exam?

To pass the Civil Service Examination (CSE), an examinee must obtain a general rating of at least 80%.

This means that your overall transmuted score across all sections of the examination must reach or exceed the 80% passing mark established by the Civil Service Commission (CSC).

The 80% passing requirement applies to both:

  • Professional Level Examination
  • Subprofessional Level Examination

Passing the exam grants Civil Service Eligibility, which is a key requirement when applying for permanent government positions in the Philippines.

How Is the Civil Service Exam Scored?

The Civil Service Commission computes your overall examination rating based on your performance across all core subject areas. The examination typically covers:

  • Verbal Ability (English and Filipino)
  • Numerical Ability (Basic Mathematics and Word Problems)
  • Analytical Ability (Professional Level only)
  • Clerical Ability (Subprofessional Level only)
  • General Information (Philippine Constitution, Code of Conduct, Peace and Environment Concepts)

Your final rating is determined by your total score across the entire test and not solely on whether you passed an individual subject area. Because of this, examinees should aim to perform well overall rather than focusing only on their strongest subjects.

How Many Questions Must You Answer Correctly?

The actual scoring method of the CSC uses a statistical transmutation system rather than a simple raw percentage. However, to ensure a safe pass, examinees can guide their preparation using these item breakdowns:

Professional Level: Has a total of 170 items. However, the first 20 questions are part of the Examinee Descriptive Questionnaire (EDQ) and are non-graded. Only 150 items are graded. To achieve an 80% rating, you generally need a target raw score of approximately 120 out of 150 correct answers.


Subprofessional Level: Has a total of 165 items (145 graded items + 20 non-graded EDQ items). To achieve an 80% rating, you generally need a target raw score of approximately 116 out of 145 correct answers.

Is 80% Difficult to Achieve?

The difficulty depends heavily on preparation and familiarity with the exam format. Many first-time examinees struggle because they underestimate the exam, spend little time reviewing, or focus purely on memorization without practicing time management.

Successful examinees often maximize their chances by doing the following:

  • Studying consistently weeks before the exam.
  • Taking full-length mock examinations to build pacing.
  • Reviewing previous question patterns and logic structures.
  • Strengthening weak subject areas (especially numerical and word problems).

What Happens If You Pass?

Passing the Civil Service Examination grants permanent Civil Service Eligibility (Second Level Eligibility for Professional, First Level Eligibility for Subprofessional). This certificate can be used when applying for permanent public sector roles, subject to the qualification standards of the specific government position.

What Happens If You Fail?

Examinees who do not achieve the 80% passing mark can retake the examination during future schedules. The CSC allows retakes provided that you do not take the exact same level of examination within a three-month period. Many successful public servants passed the exam only after multiple attempts; failing once simply highlights which areas require more thorough preparation next time.

Tips to Achieve the Civil Service Exam Passing Score

  1. Focus on Weak Subjects: Dedicate more study hours to topics where you consistently score lower during mock practice sheets.
  2. Take Full-Length Mock Exams: Simulating the actual test length helps build standard test-taking endurance and essential time control.
  3. Practice Daily: Consistency trumps cramming. Spending 30 to 60 minutes reviewing everyday leads to significant analytical improvements over a few months.
  4. Improve Reading Comprehension: A huge percentage of the exam features lengthy situational text. Speed reading and comprehension help save valuable minutes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Passing Score the Same for Professional and Subprofessional Exams?
Yes. Both variants require an overall transmuted general rating of at least 80% to pass.
Does Passing Guarantee a Government Job?
No. Passing grants you official Civil Service Eligibility. It makes you legally eligible to apply for permanent jobs, but you must still pass the background, interview, and education requirements of the hiring agency.
Are all 170 items graded in the Professional Exam?
No. The first 20 items are part of the Examinee Descriptive Questionnaire (EDQ) which gathers basic personal data. Only the remaining 150 items are computed for your official grade.

Final Thoughts

The 80% passing threshold requires a balanced approach to the test. Rather than gambling on pure guesswork, focus on developing strong operational skills across verbal, mathematical, analytical, and general knowledge concepts. Regular utilization of mock evaluation tools can systematically pull your practice scores closer to that winning target rating.
